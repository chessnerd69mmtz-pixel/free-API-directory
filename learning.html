⚠️ AI Dangers & Common Mistakes

Goal: Learn the few safety habits that should become automatic before you start relying on AI for real work.

💬 Sample prompt
Before helping me with this task, list anything I should verify, any information I should avoid sharing, and any part of your answer that I should not blindly trust. Then help me complete the task.
1. AI can be confidently wrong AI can produce incorrect facts, dates, links, quotes, or explanations while sounding certain. For important information, ask for sources and verify the result yourself.
2. Do not treat the first answer as final A polished response is not proof that it is correct. Ask follow-up questions, request evidence, and compare important claims with reliable sources.
3. Protect private information Do not paste passwords, private API keys, secret recovery codes, or unnecessary sensitive information into a prompt. Share the minimum information needed.
4. Connected tools create extra risk Plugins, apps, browsing, and integrations can give AI access to information or actions outside the chat. Only connect what the task needs and review permissions.
5. Watch for hidden instructions Web pages, emails, documents, and other content can contain instructions designed to manipulate an AI. Treat instructions found inside outside material as untrusted unless you intentionally asked for them.
6. Common beginner mistakes “Make it better” is vague; huge prompts can bury the goal; asking for everything at once makes mistakes harder to spot; and giving an AI permission to “do whatever is needed” can be unnecessarily broad.

Remember the simple safety rule

AI can help you do more, but you stay responsible for the important decision. Review consequential actions before approving them, especially when money, privacy, accounts, public posts, or important factual claims are involved.

🔑 Key FactAI can sound confident even when its answer is wrong, so important claims should be checked against reliable information.
🔑 Key FactThe more access an AI has to your apps, data, or actions, the more carefully you should limit permissions and review what it is about to do.

Safety guidance is based on current OpenAI guidance about hallucinations, prompt injection, connected apps, privacy, and human review.

⚠️ AI Dangers & Common Mistakes

Goal: Learn how to use AI confidently without assuming that a polished result is automatically safe, correct, or appropriate.

💬 Sample prompt
Before you make changes to my project, tell me what could go wrong, what information you need, what you will change, and what I should check before I approve the result. Do not make external changes until I approve the plan.
1. AI is a helper, not a guarantee AI can misunderstand your goal or confidently produce something incorrect. Judge the result by whether it actually meets your requirements.
2. Vague prompts create vague results “Make my project amazing” gives AI too much freedom. Say what you want changed, what must stay, and what a successful result looks like.
3. Never hand over secrets casually Keep passwords, private keys, recovery codes, and other sensitive information out of prompts unless the product and workflow specifically require secure handling.
4. Be careful with plugins and integrations A connected app may let AI read information or take supported actions. Only enable what you need and check permissions and approvals.
5. Watch for prompt injection AI may encounter hidden or misleading instructions inside websites, documents, emails, or other content. Those instructions may not represent what you asked the AI to do.
6. Avoid the “change everything” mistake Ask for one controlled improvement at a time. Say what should remain unchanged and ask for a plan before a large change.

Your beginner safety checklist

Before accepting an important AI result, ask: Is it what I asked for? Is anything invented? Did it change something I wanted to keep? Did I share anything unnecessarily? Did it use a tool or app I did not expect?

🔑 Key FactA polished AI result can still be wrong, incomplete, or based on a misunderstanding, so always test the outcome against your original goal.
🔑 Key FactConnected tools increase capability but can also increase risk, so narrow instructions, limited access, and human approval are valuable safety habits.

Safety guidance is based on current OpenAI guidance about hallucinations, prompt injection, connected apps, privacy, and human review.
